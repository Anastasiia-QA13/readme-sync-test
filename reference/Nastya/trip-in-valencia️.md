Valencia is a major city in eastern Spain, known for its rich history, cultural attractions, and blend of old and new architecture. Founded by the Romans in 138 BC, it was later influenced by Visigothic and Moorish rule before the Christian reconquest in 1245. Today, it is the capital of its autonomous community, a cultural hub, and the birthplace of paella.
History
Roman founding: The city was founded as Valentia Edetanorum by Roman consul Decimus Junius Brutus Callaicus in 138 BC, named for the Roman soldiers who fought for the area.
Moors and Christians: After the Roman Empire's decline, Valencia was ruled by Visigoths and then by Moors for centuries. The Moors significantly developed the city, contributing to its prosperity through trade in silk and spices. The Crown of Aragon completed the Christian reconquest in 1245.
Kingdom of Valencia: The city became the capital of the independent Kingdom of Valencia, which had its own parliament and laws, the Furs de València.
Modern era: The kingdom's autonomy was abolished in 1707, but the region has continued to advocate for autonomy in modern times.
Culture and attractions
Architecture: Valencia is a city of contrasts, featuring historic buildings alongside futuristic architecture. The modern, multi-building City of Arts and Sciences complex, designed by Santiago Calatrava, is a prime example of this modern side.
Historic center: The Ciutat Vella (Old Town) is a historic area with numerous landmarks.
Valencia Cathedral: Built in the 13th century on the site of a former mosque and Roman temple, it is home to a relic claimed to be the Holy Grail.
Lonja de la Seda (Silk Exchange): A UNESCO World Heritage site and one of Europe's most treasured Gothic buildings, it reflects the city's past wealth from the silk trade.
Gastronomy: Valencia is the birthplace of paella. Visitors can also explore the Mercado Central, one of Europe's largest markets.
Geography and demographics
Location: Valencia is located on the Mediterranean coast at the mouth of the Turia River.
Population: It is the third-largest city in Spain, with a population of over 800,000 people in the city proper.
