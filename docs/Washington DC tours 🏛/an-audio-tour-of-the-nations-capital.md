Washington, D.C.: The Nation's Capital
Washington, D.C., is the capital of the United States of America. It is located on the Potomac River and is not part of any state but is its own federal district, the District of Columbia. It was established in 1791 to serve as the nation's seat of government.
The city is a major center of political power, culture, and history. It is home to the three branches of the federal government:
The White House, where the President of the United States lives and works.
The U.S. Capitol Building, where the Senate and House of Representatives meet.
The Supreme Court Building.
Besides its role in government, Washington, D.C., is also a vibrant metropolis and a popular tourist destination. The National Mall, a large, open park, is surrounded by many of the city's most famous monuments and museums. Visitors can see the Lincoln Memorial, the Washington Monument, and the Jefferson Memorial.
The city also hosts the world-renowned Smithsonian Institution, which comprises 19 museums and a zoo, offering free admission to all. A prominent feature of the city is the National Cherry Blossom Festival, an annual event celebrating the beautiful cherry trees gifted by Japan in 1912.
