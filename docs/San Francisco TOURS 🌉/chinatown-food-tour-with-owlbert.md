## For Visitors and Locals!

<Image border={false} src="https://media.timeout.com/images/105813572/image.jpg" />

Chinatowns around the world are vibrant culinary hubs, celebrated for offering a wide array of authentic and fusion Chinese dishes, often reflecting the specific regional origins of their immigrant communities, primarily Cantonese cuisine in Western countries. The food experience in these neighborhoods is a sensory journey, from the aromas of street food stalls to the bustling atmosphere of traditional restaurants.
Common Foods and Culinary Delights
Dim Sum: A cornerstone of the Chinatown experience, dim sum refers to a meal of various small, bite-sized portions, typically served in stackable bamboo steamers. Popular items include pork and shrimp dumplings (siew mai), soft and fluffy steamed buns (baozi or char siu bao), rice noodle rolls with a sweet soy sauce, and spare ribs with black beans. It is a communal dining tradition, often enjoyed with tea on weekend mornings.
Noodle Dishes: Hand-pulled noodles and those served in rich broths are a staple. Variations include spicy Dan Dan noodles from Sichuan and thick BiangBiang noodles from Xi'an.
Peking Duck: Considered a national dish in China, Peking duck is an iconic offering in many high-end Chinatown restaurants, known for its incredibly crispy, lacquered skin and tender meat, often served with a rich plum sauce.
